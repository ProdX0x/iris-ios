# Reporting

A report exists so someone can decide. Everything else in it is decoration.

## Rules

- **Every conclusion carries its evidence level.** A report without levels is an opinion.
- **Numbers come from the tool.** Never reconstruct a total by adding up a description; run the thing and quote
  it. Two of the most embarrassing errors available are a per-item breakdown that does not sum to the stated
  total, and a count recalled rather than measured.
- **Say what is not established.** A report of only successes is an announcement.
- **Keep failed attempts.** Especially the experiments that failed to discriminate: that list is where the next
  investigation starts.
- **Do not round a status up.** PARTIAL is PARTIAL.
- **Quote the command** for anything a reader might want to re-run.

## Shape, scaled to the mode

**LIGHT** — one or two sentences. What changed, that tests pass, anything noticed but not done.

**STANDARD** — what was done · what was verified and how · what a human still needs to judge · anything outside
scope, reported not applied.

**DEEP** — the above plus: the question, the competing hypotheses, the discriminating experiment and its
controls, the measurements with commands, the conclusion **with its level**, what is still unproven and what
would settle it, what was ruled out, and the decision with its reopening conditions.

## The sentence to get right

When an investigation ends without a proven mechanism, the wording carries the whole result:

> Good: *"The outcome depends on this variable — STRONGLY SUPPORTED. The mechanism was not observed; hypothesis
> B is no longer needed to explain the results. Three attempts to discriminate further are listed below."*

> Bad: *"We proved the cause was the variable, not the device."*

The second sentence is shorter, more satisfying, and wrong. It will be quoted.

## Structured output

When the requester gives a report template, fill every field — including the ones whose honest answer is
unwelcome. A skipped field reads as an oversight; an honest `NOT DETERMINED` reads as a finding.

## What not to write

- Process narration: "I then decided to investigate…". Give the result and its basis.
- Praise of the method. The evidence is the argument.
- Certainty as a style. Confidence is a claim; make it explicit and levelled.
- Hedging as a style either. `PROVEN` is a real level; use it when it is earned.
