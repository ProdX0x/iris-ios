# 05 — Machine evidence and human evidence kept apart

## The situation

A release step changed how a gaze marker was presented, added a learning sequence, reworded a calibration
message and added feedback to one level. Much of it was measurable. Some of it was not.

## The split

The machine established: the exact wording of every label; that the long explanations were not replaced by the
short ones; that one rule produced both the drawn text and the accessibility value, so they could not diverge;
that both variants fit the usable width of every supported device up to the largest accessibility text size; and
that long sentences grew taller as text grew — the signature of wrapping rather than clipping.

The person was asked only for what remained: whether a fade is perceptible, whether a mode is recognisable while
playing, whether an enlarged panel is still *comfortable*, and what a screen reader actually says.

The checklist named both halves, so the person was not asked to re-verify what was already proven.

## Recording the verdict

The person returned a single global verdict: it passed. The checklist had eighteen points.

They were recorded as **passed as a whole**, with a note that item-level results had not been reported. Expanding
one verdict into eighteen ticks would have fabricated seventeen results.

## The third state

One accessibility technology was not tested. It was recorded neither as passed nor as failed, but as **out of
criterion** for that release, with the reason — the product is played by looking at the screen — and three
explicit statements of what that does **not** license: no claim of accessibility for blind players, no removal
of the technology, no degradation of what exists.

## Lessons

1. Machine and human evidence are two domains, not two grades. Neither outranks the other.
2. Tell the person what is already proven. Their attention is the scarce resource.
3. Record a verdict at the grain it was given. Inventing detail is inventing evidence.
4. "Out of criterion" is a legitimate third state — and it needs its reason **and** its limits, or it becomes a
   way to make awkward criteria disappear.
5. An installation is not a visual validation. A build is not a judgement.

## What this does not establish

That the split was drawn in the right place. It was drawn by judgement, and a different person might move it.
